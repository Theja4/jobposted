# Product

## Register

product

## Users

JobPosted serves active job seekers who want more relevant applications out in the market without handing control to a spray-and-pray automation tool. They may be applying from a laptop during focused job-search sessions, checking a review queue between interviews, or tuning preferences when the feed gets noisy.

Primary users need to find matched roles, tailor a resume for each one, queue applications, review filled forms, and explicitly confirm each submission. They expect the interface to feel efficient, trustworthy, and clear about what has and has not been sent.

## Product Purpose

JobPosted is an AI job-application copilot. It finds matching roles, uses Rezoomed's tailoring engine to prepare a role-specific resume, fills supported application forms, and parks every submission for user confirmation before anything is sent.

Success means users submit fewer but better applications, understand why each role is recommended, keep control of sensitive accounts such as LinkedIn and Workday, and trust that the system will never submit behind their back.

## Brand Personality

Trustworthy, direct, and quietly capable.

The voice is plainspoken and candid: "Copilot, not autopilot"; "we never spray"; "you confirm every submission." JobPosted should feel like a careful operations tool for a serious job search, with enough warmth to reduce anxiety but no hype that makes automation feel reckless.

## Anti-references

Do not make JobPosted look or sound like a mass auto-apply spam tool, a growth-hack dashboard, or an aggressive AI agent that runs without consent.

Avoid dark neon AI-console aesthetics, purple/blue gradient SaaS gloss, glassmorphism, fake "magic" claims, oversized decorative metrics, and anything that implies LinkedIn or login-gated platforms run from JobPosted servers. Do not bury confirmation, privacy, or user-control language behind marketing flourish.

## Design Principles

1. Keep user control visible. Every application flow should reinforce that the user reviews and confirms before submission.
2. Optimize for confident scanning. Job cards, queues, settings, and billing should expose status, match quality, apply path, and next action without visual drama.
3. Borrow trust from restraint. Use the blue brand system sparingly for primary actions, positive status, and selected navigation rather than decoration.
4. Show the workflow, not the fantasy. Product visuals should demonstrate queue, tailoring, filled form, review, and confirmation instead of abstract AI imagery.
5. Be honest about boundaries. Server-side apply, extension-based apply, data use, limits, and failures should be named plainly.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA for contrast, focus visibility, keyboard access, and form semantics. Preserve the existing reduced-motion behavior and keep motion limited to state feedback, lightweight reveals, and loading transitions.

Design for stressed users: clear labels, recoverable errors, obvious status text, no color-only meaning, and no hidden destructive or irreversible actions.
